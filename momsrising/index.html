Bottom line up front

The MomsRising audience is not asking for polished creator content. It is signaling a preference for content that combines civic relevance, women-centered authority, parenting reality, and explainer clarity.

Recommendation: Soapboxx should help MomsRising make member-generated social content that feels like warm authority — credible first-person stories, framed with clear stakes, a concrete proof point, and an easy-to-share insight. The data points away from generic testimonials and toward receipt-driven stories, explainer-style reactions, and solidarity content that sounds smart, practical, and human.

What this analysis is doing

This is an audience-environment study. It does not directly tell us what creative will win, but it gives a strong signal about what already feels native, credible, and worth following to MomsRising’s broader audience.

Method

We built a cross-platform StatSocial profile of people who follow MomsRising across Instagram, X, YouTube, and Bluesky, then used inverse network analysis to identify the other accounts, hashtags, and descriptors those followers are more likely than average to follow. In practical terms, this reveals the audience’s organic taste environment.

How to use it

Use the findings to shape content style briefs, prompt design, and testing hypotheses. Treat high-affinity accounts and hashtags as creative clues: they reveal tone, format, framing, authority style, and what kinds of stories or explainers already feel at home in the audience’s feed.

Important caveat: inverse-follow analysis is directional, not dispositive. Some tails are noisy, especially on smaller-platform slices. The best use is to form clear creative hypotheses and then test them against real post performance.

Main findings

The strongest recurring signal is an audience that lives at the intersection of policy literacy, care work, culture, and explanation.

1. Progressive civic action, but with receipts

X and TikTok are rich with movement, issue, and public-interest media signals. This audience follows advocacy and politics, but it leans toward explainers, reactions, and concrete lived evidence rather than abstract talking points.

2. Women-centered authority

Instagram especially points to a strong affinity for women public figures, parenting ecosystems, and women’s media. The subtext is important: motherhood and caregiving can be voiced as public expertise, not just personal emotion.

3. Smart, warm, culturally literate

The audience also follows entertainment and culture voices with warmth and point of view. That suggests MomsRising should not sound like a dry institution. It should sound like a trusted, informed, human presence in the feed.

4. Explainer-ready

TED, NPR, PBS, public-interest news, and reaction-oriented hashtags suggest that concise framing works. Good content for this audience likely answers: What happened, why it matters, and what people miss.

5. Network amplifiers in the base

Job-title signals over-index toward executive directors, founders, authors, writers, and communications roles. Some of the follower base likely has downstream influence and may share content that makes them look informed, useful, and values-aligned.

Working interpretation:
MomsRising’s content should sit in the overlap between advocacy, care, competence, and humanity.

That means: real-member testimony, social-native framing, useful sentences, and direct emotional truth without overproduced creator cosplay.

Implications for the Soapboxx content brief

These are the creative lanes most supported by the audience-affinity data. Each one can be turned into a Soapboxx prompt, collection workflow, and post template.

What to lean into
  • Direct-to-camera vertical video.
  • One idea per clip, with captions always on.
  • A strong first sentence that names the stake, not the issue category.
  • Specific, repeatable language people can quote or stitch.
  • Stories that move from lived moment → meaning → implication.
What to avoid
  • Open-ended “tell us your story” asks that produce shapeless submissions.
  • Polished influencer mimicry that feels borrowed from a different audience.
  • Institutional script language or over-briefed advocacy jargon.
  • Long preambles that delay the actual human point.
  • Assuming emotional warmth means a lack of policy seriousness.
Suggested testing plan

For the proof of concept, test 4–6 archetypes rather than “UGC” as a single bucket: receipt stories, warm-authority explainers, reaction/stitch responses, solidarity confessionals, one-stat one-story clips, and organizer-forward calls to action. Judge results not only on views, but on saves, shares, watch-through, comment quality, repeat contributor rate, and how often the content gives supporters a sentence they want to reuse.

Appendix

A few practical notes for interpreting the tables.

Overlap

How many MomsRising followers are also connected to that account, hashtag, or title in the modeled audience.

Index

Relative lift versus average. Very high values can be useful, but can also become noisy on niche entities, so they should be read with scale in mind.

Similarity

A helpful balancing metric because it rewards affinity while still reflecting scale. For creative strategy, it is often more useful than pure index alone.
